STORY 6 — The Night of the Missing Noise
Harrowverse Domestic Episode

It started on a Thursday evening, the kind of evening where nothing dramatic is supposed to happen. Jean was in the kitchen making tea. Jeff was on the sofa pretending to read but mostly scrolling. Astra was standing in the hallway in his usual “resting sentinel” posture, hands folded, eyes soft, like a man waiting for instructions that would never come. Buster was asleep in a position that suggested he had collapsed mid‑thought. And Edenshell was doing what it always did at that hour: a low, steady hum, the house’s version of breathing.

Then the hum stopped.

Not faded.  
Not softened.  
Stopped.

The silence was immediate and absolute, like someone had pressed mute on the entire building. Jean froze with the kettle halfway to the mug. Jeff looked up from his phone. Astra tilted his head slightly, as if listening for something that should have been there but wasn’t.

“Did the power go?” Jean asked.

The lights were still on.  
The warmth was still there.  
The house still felt alive — just… quiet.

Too quiet.

Jeff stood up slowly, the way people do when they’re not sure if they’re overreacting. “Maybe it’s doing a reset,” he said, though he had no idea if Edenshell could reset. Astra walked to the centre of the living room and stood very still, like a man trying to sense a change in atmospheric pressure.

Buster woke up, looked around, and immediately barked at the silence, as if offended by it.

Jean put the kettle down. “I don’t like this,” she said. “It feels wrong.”

And she was right.  
The house without its hum felt like a person holding their breath.

---

Jeff checked the fuse box.  
Astra checked the hallway.  
Jean checked the oven for reasons nobody understood.  
Buster followed everyone, barking at each location equally.

Nothing was broken.  
Nothing was wrong.  
The house was simply… quiet.

Edenshell’s lights stayed steady, but the usual soft drift — the tiny shifts in warmth, the subtle changes in airflow — were gone. The house felt like it was waiting for something.

Jean sat on the sofa, arms folded. “I don’t like when things go quiet,” she said. “It feels like the start of a horror film.”

Jeff sat beside her. “It’s not a horror film,” he said, though he didn’t sound convinced.

Astra remained standing in the centre of the room, perfectly still, as if he were trying to model the silence. Buster sat next to him, staring at the wall with the intensity of a dog who believed he could intimidate architecture.

---

It lasted twenty‑three minutes.

Then, without warning, Edenshell exhaled.

A soft warmth moved through the room.  
The lights shifted by a fraction of a shade.  
A low hum returned, gentle and steady, like a creature waking up from a long blink.

Jean let out a breath she didn’t realise she’d been holding.  
Jeff relaxed his shoulders.  
Astra straightened slightly, as if the world had returned to its correct orientation.  
Buster barked once, satisfied that he had personally resolved the situation.

The house settled back into its usual rhythm, the kind of rhythm you only notice when it disappears.

Jean stood up and said, “Right. I’m making that tea now.”

Jeff nodded. “Good idea.”

Astra resumed his place in the hallway.  
Buster lay down again, this time in a position that suggested he had won something.

Edenshell hummed softly, as if apologising.

---

Later that night, as they were getting ready for bed, Jean said, “I know it’s just a house, but when it went quiet… it felt like it left.”

Jeff brushed his teeth, thought about it, and said, “Yeah. It did.”

Edenshell dimmed the lights by a fraction, the smallest gesture of reassurance it could offer.

And the house breathed again.
