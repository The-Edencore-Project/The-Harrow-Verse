# STORY 7 — The Argument in the Garden
Harrowverse Domestic Episode

It happened on a Sunday afternoon, the kind of afternoon where the house felt slow and the air outside had that early‑spring chill that made everyone slightly more irritable than usual. Sarah had come home for the weekend. Elliot was with her, carrying her bags with the quiet determination of a man who wanted to make a good impression. Callum arrived not long after, unannounced but not unexpected, the way he always did — as if the Harrow house were a second home he never officially moved out of.

The trouble started small.  
It always does.

Callum made a comment about Elliot’s shoes — something about them being “very… university.” Elliot laughed politely, not sure if it was a joke. Sarah rolled her eyes. Jeff pretended not to hear. Jean, sensing tension, offered everyone tea in the way mothers do when they want to prevent a situation from becoming a situation.

But the air didn’t settle.  
It thickened.

Callum followed Elliot into the garden under the pretext of “helping him bring in the chairs,” which was strange because Elliot hadn’t asked for help and the chairs didn’t need bringing in. Sarah watched from the kitchen window, arms folded, already annoyed at both of them for reasons she couldn’t articulate.

In the garden, the conversation started civil.  
Then it tilted.

Callum said something about “knowing Sarah longer.”  
Elliot said something about “not trying to replace anyone.”  
Callum said something about “not being replaced.”  
Elliot said something about “just wanting things to be calm.”

And then, in the most Harrowverse way possible, they had a fight that wasn’t really a fight — more of a raised‑voice misunderstanding between two men who didn’t actually dislike each other but had accidentally wandered into emotional territory neither was equipped to navigate.

It lasted about ninety seconds.

Buster barked at them once, loudly, as if issuing a formal warning.  
Both men stopped immediately.

Jean opened the back door and said, “If you’re going to argue, at least bring the chairs in like you said you would.”  
That ended it.

Callum apologised first, in the stiff, awkward way he always apologised, like someone returning a borrowed jumper they’d accidentally shrunk. Elliot apologised back, overly earnest, overly gentle, trying to smooth the air. They shook hands in a way that suggested neither of them knew what to do with their other hand.

By the time they came inside, the tension had dissolved into something almost friendly.  
Sarah looked at them both and said, “Are you done.”  
They both nodded.

And that was that.

---

That evening, after everyone had gone home or upstairs or wherever they drifted when the day wound down, Sarah and Elliot settled in front of the fireplace. The house was quiet in that warm, late‑night way where everything feels softer. Elliot sat close, still slightly embarrassed about the garden incident. Sarah leaned against him, not saying much, just letting the fire do the talking.

Elliot wasn’t dramatic.  
He wasn’t intense.  
He wasn’t trying to prove anything.

He just sat there with her, steady and warm, the way she needed him to be.

Sarah reached for his hand.  
Elliot squeezed it gently, as if confirming he was still there.

The fire crackled.  
The house settled.  
The day, with all its small dramas, folded itself away.

For a moment, everything felt simple.
