STORY 5 — Easter Sunday (Quietly Strange)
Harrowverse Domestic Episode

Jean decided Easter needed “a bit of ceremony this year,” which is how Astra ended up standing in the kitchen at nine in the morning wearing a full‑body Easter Bunny costume. The ears leaned slightly to the left, giving him the look of someone who had accepted his role in the universe long before anyone asked his opinion. Jean handed him a basket of twenty chocolate eggs and said, with the confidence of a woman who believed in seasonal magic, “Hide these around the house. Make it gentle.”

Astra nodded once, as if this were a solemn assignment.  
Jean left to pick up pastries.  
Astra began the task.  
Buster followed.

The first egg went behind a cushion. Buster ate it.  
The second went under a chair. Buster ate it.  
The third went on a low shelf. Buster, who had never shown any interest in shelves in his entire life, suddenly discovered a new vertical ambition and ate it.

By egg six, Astra paused in the hallway, recalculated, and began placing eggs in locations that seemed, in theory, beyond Buster’s reach. Buster disproved every theory. By egg ten, Astra was moving faster. By egg fifteen, he was moving with the quiet desperation of a man trying to outrun a natural disaster. By egg twenty, he simply stood there, basket empty, costume slightly crooked, processing the situation with the dignity of someone who had done everything asked of him and still failed.

He walked outside and sat in the shed.

---

Family arrived.  
Friends arrived.  
Tommy’s mates arrived.  
Jean returned with pastries and the hopeful energy of someone expecting a wholesome Easter moment.

“Alright everyone — egg hunt.”

People scattered.  
Within a minute, the truth surfaced: there were no eggs.  
Not one.

Jeff found Buster lying on his side in the living room, belly round, eyes distant, radiating the unmistakable aura of a dog who had achieved something irreversible. Jean looked at Jeff. Jeff looked at Jean. Jean said, “Where’s Astra.” Jeff said, “Shed.”

Edenshell, sensing the rising emotional temperature, shifted into a low, steady basin. The lights softened. The air warmed slightly. A faint hum settled through the walls, the kind it used when the house wanted to keep everyone from tipping over. It didn’t fix anything. It didn’t try. It just breathed with the room.

Jean sighed, went back to the car, and returned twenty minutes later with a new bag of eggs. She opened the shed door. Astra stood up slowly, ears wilted, posture apologetic. “It’s fine,” she said. “Just… try again. And keep Buster in the garden.”

Astra nodded.

This time, the hiding worked.  
This time, Buster watched through the glass, betrayed by physics.  
The kids found eggs.  
The adults found eggs.  
Tommy found one in a shoe and declared it “deeply symbolic.”

Jean relaxed.  
Jeff ate a pastry.  
Astra stood in the corner, hands folded, like a rabbit‑shaped sentinel who had survived a difficult morning.  
Edenshell drifted into a Settled basin, tension easing through the walls like a long exhale.

---

Easter Sunday was, in its own quiet, strange way,  
a success.
