🧠 Complexity Hierarchy — Concept Note

#️⃣ Overview

This document explores the relative complexity of physics, chemistry, biology, AI, the human brain, and human interaction. It ranks each domain from least to most complex and explains why human interaction sits at the top.

⚛️ 1. The Laws of Physics

Physics is elegant and minimal. A small set of forces and constants governs everything. It is powerful but not complex in structure. It is foundational rather than intricate.

🧪 2. The Laws of Chemistry

Chemistry is physics expressed through bonds, reactions, and energy states. It is more layered than physics but still rule‑based and predictable within its constraints.

🧬 3. The Laws of Biology

Biology is chemistry that evolved. It introduces adaptation, evolution, metabolism, and ecosystems. It is messy, emergent, and full of exceptions, marking the first major jump in complexity.

🤖 4. AI and LLMs

AI systems are mathematically enormous but not alive. They rely on patterns, probability, and optimisation. They are complex machines, not complex organisms, and lack self‑directed emergence.

🧠 5. The Human Brain

The brain contains billions of neurons and trillions of synapses. It is adaptive, plastic, emotional, predictive, and self‑modifying. It is the most complex physical object we know.

🧩 6. Human Interaction (Most Complex)

Human interaction multiplies complexity. Multiple brains collide with history, emotion, culture, trauma, humour, timing, and subtext. This creates emergent behaviour that no single system can fully predict.
